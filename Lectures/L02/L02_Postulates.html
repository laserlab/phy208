1881: the first try

  • Berlin, Helmholtz’s lab. Michelson is a 28-year-old US Navy officer on leave.
  • Arms \(L \approx 1.2\) m → predicted shift 0.04 fringes
  • Berlin traffic drowned the fringes → moved to a cellar in Potsdam. Stamping on the pavement 100 m away still shifted them.
  • Result: nothing. And nobody was convinced.

The instrument was paid for by Alexander Graham Bell.

In their own words

“The Experiments on the relative motion of the earth and ether have been completed and the result decidedly negative.”

— Michelson to Lord Rayleigh, August 1887

He considered the experiment a failure, and moved on to other work.

“The most famous failed experiment in history”

It was not a failure.

Michelson himself was disappointed — he considered it a failure for years.

He was wrong about his own result.

A null result from an instrument sensitive enough to have seen the effect is a discovery.

What became of it

  • 1907 — America’s first science Nobel: “for his optical precision instruments…” The ether experiment is not mentioned.
  • 1902–04 — Morley and Miller do the seasonal repeats, bigger. Null.
  • 1920s — Miller, alone on Mount Wilson, claims a positive signal.
  • 1955 — traced to temperature gradients. Modern repeats: null, to parts in \(10^{18}\).

“Without your work, this theory would today be scarcely more than an interesting speculation.”

— Einstein, toasting Michelson, January 1931

Caltech, January 1931 — Michelson, Einstein, Millikan. Michelson died four months later.

What a null result destroys

No detectable ether wind — so something in the chain that predicted 0.4 fringes is false. There are only three ways out:

1. “Earth drags the ether along with it.” Then there is no wind at ground level, and the fringes rightly stay put. ✗ But dragged ether would also carry starlight along — and stellar aberration (Bradley’s tilted telescopes, from Tuesday) exists only if Earth moves through an undisturbed ether. One escape, two contradictory experiments.

2. “Motion through the ether squeezes the arms.” FitzGerald (1889) and Lorentz (1892): if the along-wind arm contracts by exactly \(\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}\), the two delays cancel and the null is explained. ad hoc — a factor invented to erase this one experiment, with no mechanism and no independent prediction. But hold onto that factor: it is nearly right, for entirely different reasons — week 4.

3. “There is no ether.” Light needs no medium, and \(c\) is the same in every inertial frame. Einstein, 1905 — the only escape that predicts new physics instead of explaining away old data.

Here is the story every documentary and most textbooks tell:

Michelson–Morley destroyed the ether — so Einstein invented relativity.

Is that what happened?

Einstein, 1905

Bern, Switzerland. Age 26.

  • Not a professor. A patent examiner, third class.
  • Reviews patent applications — many for electromagnetic devices and clock synchronization.
  • In one year, 1905, publishes four papers. Any one of them would have made a career.

What Einstein actually wrote

“It is known that Maxwell’s electrodynamics — as usually understood at the present time — when applied to moving bodies, leads to asymmetries which do not appear to be inherent in the phenomena.”

— Zur Elektrodynamik bewegter Körper, opening sentence, 1905

Not a word about Michelson–Morley. The paper mentions “unsuccessful attempts to discover any motion of the earth relative to the light medium” only in passing, a paragraph later.

The asymmetry that bothered him

Magnet moves, coil at rest

Changing \(\mathbf{B}\) → induced electric field → current flows

Coil moves, magnet at rest

Charges move through \(\mathbf{B}\) → force \(q\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}\) → current flows

Same current. Same experiment. Two completely different explanations.

Two postulates

1. The laws of physics are the same in every inertial frame.

2. The speed of light in vacuum is the same in every inertial frame, regardless of the motion of the source or the observer.

The first is old — Galileo would have signed it.

The second is the radical one.

Postulate 1 is 273 years older

“Shut yourself up with some friend in the main cabin below decks on some large ship … have the ship proceed with any speed you like, so long as the motion is uniform … You will discover not the least change in all the effects named, nor could you tell from any of them whether the ship was moving or standing still.”

— Galileo, Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems, 1632

Something has to give

Speed is \(\dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}\).

If everyone measures the same speed for light, but they are moving relative to each other…

Hendrik Lorentz

Wrote down the transformation equations (1892–1904) — before Einstein.

Believed length contraction was a real physical compression of matter moving through the ether.

“The chief cause of my failure was my clinging to the idea that the variable \(t\) only can be considered as the true time, and that my local time \(t'\) must be regarded as no more than an auxiliary mathematical quantity.”

— Lorentz, The Theory of Electrons, 1915 edition

Henri Poincaré

Argued simultaneity is a convention. Saw the group structure. And in September 1904 — a year early — said this:

“… the principle of relativity, according to which the laws of physical phenomena should be the same, whether for an observer fixed, or for an observer carried along in a uniform movement of translation.”

— Poincaré, address at St. Louis, September 1904

So why Einstein?

Lorentz had the mathematics. Poincaré had the principle, and the name.

They had the mathematics. Einstein supplied the meaning: no ether, no mechanism — just two postulates and the courage to follow them.
